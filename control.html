Control

Controller design, tuning and tracking results

References to the tutorial material

The 2-state error model (Weeks 5 D, 8 B), LQR with ct.dlqr and Bryson's rule (Week 6 A.4, A.5), the Lyapunov heading law (Week 6 B.6), the "drive and turn" placeholder used as a baseline (Week 8 C.4), and position-based visual servoing (PBVS) to an ArUco tag (Week 9 D). The occupancy grid and A* planner are not from the tutorials. They are a researched minimal extension so that the robot can navigate with no map as required for hardware. Code: control.py, navigation.py, planning.py. Evaluated by tests/test_control.py and tests/test_navigation.py.

Everything on this page runs on the estimated pose and sensor data ONLY.

2. Planning on the robot's own LiDAR map

  • Grid: log-odds occupancy in {A}, 5 cm cells. Each return adds +0.85 to its cell, and the cells along the beam −0.4 (limits −2 and +3.5; occupied above 0.5). It is placed using the estimated pose and restarted if a correction moves the estimate more than 5 cm in one step, because a map drawn from a wrong pose leaves ghost walls.
  • A* (8-connected): cells within 0.30 m (robot radius + 5 cm) of an occupied cell are blocked, and cells within 0.6 m cost up to 3× more so paths keep to open space. Unknown cells count as free, so the robot plans optimistically and replans as it sees more.
  • Path to spline: A* goes to a point 0.3 m back along the dock normal, and the last 0.3 m is straight, so the robot arrives lined up. The path is shortened by line of sight and then splined. The spline must keep 0.28 m clear of every return and have curvature no tighter than 15 /m. Otherwise the planner takes fewer shortcuts and as a last resort uses straight legs with turns on the spot.
  • Replanning happens on each LiDAR scan (5 Hz), but only when the path ahead is now closer to a return than when it was planned. The planner also replans when the estimate is more than 0.15 m off the path or when the safety corridor has stopped the robot for 0.5 s.

3. LQR path following

On the 2-state cross-track/heading model (A), discretised by zero-order hold at T = 0.05 s:

\[ \dot e_y = v\,e_\theta, \qquad \dot e_\theta = \omega - \omega_r, \qquad \omega = v\kappa - K(v)\begin{bmatrix} e_y \\ e_\theta \end{bmatrix}. \]
  • Feed-forward \(v\kappa\) from the spline's curvature so curves are driven rather than corrected.
  • Bryson's rule (Week 6 A.5): ey max 50 mm, eθ max 10°, steering effort max 0.5 rad/s, giving Q = diag(400.0, 32.8) and R = 4.0.
  • Gain-scheduled on speed: v appears inside A (Week 5 D) so K is computed with ct.dlqr for the speed being driven and cached per cm/s.
  • Speed: v = min(0.15, 0.8/κmax, max(0.04, 0.6 × distance to the end or the next corner)). 0.15 m/s is 66% of the 0.227 m/s top speed, which leaves headroom for steering; κmax is the largest curvature in the next 0.2 m.
  • Turn on the spot whenever |eθ| > 30°, outside the linear model's range. It uses the Lyapunov law \(\omega = -k e_\theta\) with k = 1.5: V = e²/2 gives \(\dot V = -k e^2 < 0\), and it exits below 3°.
  • LiDAR safety: v = ω = 0 if any return lies in a 0.41 m-wide corridor out to 0.29 m ahead.

The loop is stable at every speed, and both poles are real, meaning there is no overshoot. The slow, cross-track mode's time constant scales with 1/v - the robot can only correct a lateral error by driving, which is the nonholonomic constraint again. The fast heading mode (about 0.35 s) barely depends on speed. Both are well above the wheels' 0.11 s lag.

The reason for Bryson's choice: A 100× more aggressive controller cuts settling only from 6.05 to 5.80 sec, but multiplies the peak steering command 5.5×, to 5.07 rad/s. That is beyond the 1.58 rad/s that the wheels can deliver. Cross-track recovery is limited by forward speed, not steering effort. Every design settles within 0.15 s of the linear prediction with the lag included so leaving the lag out of the model is justified.

4. Visual docking (Week 9 D3)

At the pre-dock pose the robot turns to face the station, then steers on the camera's view of the dock marker (not the EKF):

\[ v = \lambda_v (z_M - d^*), \qquad \omega = -\lambda_\omega \operatorname{atan2}(x_M, z_M), \qquad \lambda_v = 0.5,\ \lambda_\omega = 1.5,\ d^* = 0.25\ \text{m}. \]

Here xM and zM are the marker's lateral offset and depth in the camera frame. v is clipped to [−0.05, 0.1] m/s and ω to ±0.8 rad/s. d* is set by geometry - the marker can only be decoded while its 0.2 m plate fits in the 480 px image height, Z ≥ 0.2 × 530/480 = 0.22 m. The robot counts as docked within 10 mm in both depth and lateral offset.

A rotated start docks perfectly. A start off the normal docks facing the marker, but about half the offset remains sideways, with about 8° of skew per 100 mm. This matches the Week 9 D3 prediction: the law regulates only range and bearing and a unicycle cannot move sideways. The full mission still docks within millimetres because the LQR tracker, steering on the EKF estimate, delivers the robot to the pre-dock pose on the normal. Docking accuracy is therefore set by estimation plus tracking error at that point. That is why the explore mission adds back off when the estimate shows the robot pushed off the normal.

6. Team Integration

Anthony used a cubic-spline trajectory with curvature feed-forward, a route with a clearance check, and a 3-state LQR tracker on a timed reference. We compared both his and Kaja's straight-line before merging:

  • Merged: The spline geometry and feed-forward (src/control/trajectory.py), the route, and the clearance check.
  • Not used: The timed reference and its tracker. A timed reference keeps moving while the LiDAR has stopped the robot, so the tracking error grows without bound, and it cannot be replanned. In a pre-merge robustness run it failed 10/10 unexpected-obstacle trials. It was also discretised by forward Euler, which pushes a turning reference's eigenvalues outside the unit circle (A, section 6), and it assumed a 0.26 m/s top speed instead of 0.227 m/s.
  • Bugs found while merging (now fixed): overshooting sharp straight-leg corners and rejoining after a detour cutting into known obstacles.
Extensions beyond the tutorial material
  • A LiDAR occupancy grid with A* and replanning, so the robot navigates with no map. It is a minimal extension.
  • A* on the LiDAR occupancy grid: This is not in the tutorials. 8-connected search on 5 cm cells with a clearance-based cost (blocked within 0.30 m of a return, up to 3× dearer out to 0.6 m) and an admissible straight-line heuristic, so the path is the cheapest on the grid and keeps to open space. Unknown space is treeated as free and the robot replans as it sees more. The result is shortened and splined for the LQR tracker.
  • Gain scheduling on speed, curvature feed-forward and a curvature-limited speed law, justified by a trade-off curve and a check to ensure that the neglected lag does not matter.
  • The docking law's lateral limit predicted, measured, and handled at the mission level (back off), which took push-during-docking from 0/10 to 10/10 (E).
  • Three controllers compared on the same seeds, and merged made on measured evidence.
What these results do and do not support
  • Docking stops about 10 mm short every time, because it stops as soon as the camera reads |zM − d*| < 10 mm while approaching from outside. A tighter tolerance trades this against time, since the approach slows exponentially with a time constant 1/λv = 2 s.
  • The planner treats the robot as a 0.25 m circle on a 5 cm grid. It is untested with moving obstacles, gaps under 0.6 m, mazes, or rooms larger than its 9×9 m grid. An obstacle dropped right next to the pre-dock pose made it replan about 100 times before docking.
  • The faster spline mission collects fewer marker updates than straight segments, which is a measured trade-off against estimation margin (E).
