====================================================================================================
1. DESIGN (src/control/control.py, MissionController)
====================================================================================================
Runs on the ESTIMATED pose (EKF) and sensor data only. Three modes, plus a lidar detour planner.

1.1 The reference path (control.Path)
- A cubic spline through START_POSE and the WAYPOINTS, with the start heading set to the start pose's heading and
  the end heading set to face the dock (Anthony Fava's Trajectory geometry, src/control/trajectory.py).
- Sampled every 1 cm: position, tangent heading theta_r, curvature kappa and arc length s.
- Route (Anthony's): (-0.6, -1.3), (-0.1, -0.7), (-0.05, 0.2), (0.1, 0.85), (0.5, 1.0), (1.0, 1.0), pre-dock
  (1.1, 1.0). Spline length 4.36 m.
- Route clearance beyond the robot's radius (Anthony's check, test_control.py Part 0):
    spline (used)        length 4.36 m, 8.8 cm from obstacles, 20.0 cm from walls
    straight segments    length 4.30 m, 6.0 cm from obstacles, 20.0 cm from walls
  The previous route ((1.2, -1.45), (1.2, 0.2), (1.1, 1.0)) only cleared an obstacle by 0.9 cm when splined, which
  is why the route changed with the spline.
- smooth=False (python main.py --controller straight) gives straight segments between the same points: the
  pre-merge behaviour, kept for comparison. Detour legs are always straight (1.5).

1.2 Track: LQR path following with curvature feed-forward
- Model: the 2-state cross-track / heading error model of Weeks 5 D and 8 B,
    e_y' = v e_theta,  e_theta' = omega - omega_r   (e = actual - reference; e_y positive to the left of the path)
  discretised by zero-order hold at CONTROL_DT = 0.05 s (Week 5 C5).
- Nearest path point: searched up to 0.5 m ahead of the current one, so progress along the path is monotonic.
- Control: omega = v kappa - K(v) [e_y, e_theta]
    v kappa         curvature feed-forward (omega_r = v kappa, Anthony's), so curves are driven, not corrected
    K(v)            ct.dlqr gain (Week 6 A.4), Bryson's rule (Week 6 A.5): e_y max 50 mm, e_theta max 10 deg,
                    steering effort max 0.5 rad/s -> Q = diag(400.0, 32.8), R = 4.0. v sits inside A (Week 5 D),
                    so K is gain-scheduled on the speed being driven (cached per cm/s).
- Speed: v = min(0.15, 0.8 / kappa_max, max(0.04, 0.6 x distance to the end or the next corner))
    0.15 m/s nominal (66 % of the 0.227 m/s top speed, leaving headroom for steering)
    kappa_max = largest curvature in the next 0.2 m, so the feed-forward never asks for more than 0.8 rad/s
- Lidar safety: v = omega = 0 if any lidar return lies in the 0.41 m-wide corridor out to 0.29 m ahead.

1.3 Turn: turning on the spot
- At the start, at the corners of straight (detour) legs, before docking, and whenever |e_theta| > 30 deg (outside
  the linear model's range, Week 5 B2).
- Lyapunov heading law (Week 6 B.6): omega = -k e_theta, k = 1.5, clipped to 0.8 x top turn rate. V = e^2 / 2
  gives V' = -k e^2 < 0, so it converges for any k > 0. Exits below 3 deg.
- Corner detection: on straight legs, a heading jump of more than 30 deg within the next 1.5 cm of path switches to
  Turn and steps past the corner (bug fix, 2.4).

1.4 Dock: visual docking (Week 9 D3)
- At the pre-dock pose (1.1, 1.0), on the dock marker's normal with the camera ~0.6 m from the marker, the robot
  turns to face the station, then drives on the CAMERA's view of the dock marker (not the EKF):
    v = lam_v (z_M - d*),  omega = -lam_w atan2(x_M, z_M),  lam_v = 0.5, lam_w = 1.5 (tutorial values)
  x_M, z_M = lateral offset and depth of the marker in the camera frame (perception.docking_error).
  v clipped to [-0.05, 0.1] m/s, omega to +/-0.8 rad/s.
- d* = DOCK_DISTANCE = 0.25 m camera-to-marker. Set by geometry: the marker can only be decoded while its 0.2 m
  plate fits in the 480 px image height, Z >= 0.2 m x 530 px / 480 px = 0.22 m.
- Docked when |z_M - d*| < 10 mm and |x_M| < 10 mm (DOCK_TOL). Target docked pose: robot origin (1.459, 1.0),
  heading 0.
- No lidar stop while docking (the dock itself is the obstacle being approached). If the marker is not seen for
  0.25 s: stop and turn towards where the EKF says the marker is.

1.5 Detour around an unmapped obstacle (extension for criterion E; not from the tutorials)
- If the path stays blocked for 1 s: the obstacle = the lidar returns in the corridor ahead plus anything within
  0.3 m of them. Two new waypoints, one beside the robot and one beside the obstacle's far side (offset = the
  obstacle's lateral extent on that side + robot radius + 5 cm), on whichever side leaves more clearance. Accepted only if every new leg keeps
  the robot's radius + 2 cm from every return (the first, sideways leg only must not get closer than the robot
  already is).
- Straight legs to the first waypoint beyond the obstacle, then the rest of the planned SPLINE from that point
  (Path.join).
- Design scope: a minimal LOCAL replanner. The fixed route is planned around the known obstacles.


====================================================================================================
2. INTEGRATION OF ANTHONY FAVA'S CONTROL WORK (Anthony_Branch -> integration-branch)
====================================================================================================

2.1 What his branch had
- Trajectory: a cubic spline through the waypoints with optional start / end headings, timed at v_ref, with
  sample(t) giving the reference pose and the feed-forward (v_r, omega_r = v_r kappa).
- LQRTracker: LQR on the full 3-state error model in the robot frame (Kanayama form), controlling v and omega,
  Q = diag(40, 40, 8), R = diag(5, 2), gains from the discrete Riccati equation (forward-Euler discretisation),
  cached per (v_r, omega_r).
- ROUTE: waypoints through the middle of the room ending on the dock's normal; a route clearance check against the
  known map (mission.clearance).

2.2 Merged INTO the existing controller (not kept as an alternative)
- The spline path geometry and the curvature feed-forward omega_r = v kappa (src/control/trajectory.py, used by
  control.Path).
- His route, now config.WAYPOINTS, ending at the existing pre-dock pose.
- The clearance check (src/control/mission.py), now test_control.py Part 0.
- Kept from the existing controller: path FOLLOWING (nearest point) instead of a timed reference, turning on the
  spot, the lidar stop and detours, the EKF recovery, and Week 9 docking.

2.3 Not merged, and why
- The timed reference (Trajectory.sample) and LQRTracker. A timed reference keeps moving while the robot is stopped
  by the lidar, so the tracking error grows without bound, and it cannot be replanned around an obstacle. In the
  pre-merge robustness run his tracker failed 10/10 unexpected-obstacle trials ("blocked, no detour found").
- Forward-Euler discretisation pushes the open-loop eigenvalues of a turning reference outside the unit circle
  (findings_A_B 4.6); zero-order hold does not.
- His saturate() assumed 0.26 m/s; the usable top speed is 0.227 m/s (findings_A_B 4.2).
- Removed from the integration branch in the clean-up (unused): Trajectory.sample, LQRTracker, saturate,
  to_wheel_speeds, ROUTE / DOCK_STOP / DOCK_HEADING, and his standalone test scripts. They remain on
  Anthony_Branch.

2.4 Bugs found while merging (all fixed)
1. Overshooting sharp straight-leg corners: at a ~92 deg detour corner the nearest path point stayed AT the corner
   with the old heading, so the robot kept driving straight. Fixed by corner detection (1.3).
2. Rejoining after a detour: rejoining with straight legs through the remaining waypoints cut into known obstacles
   (the waypoints were designed for the spline). Fixed: rejoin the spline itself (Path.join).
3. Robustness obstacle placement: on the new route the obstacle scenario placed the cylinder in the 0.52 m gap
   between the yellow and green cylinders, which a 0.5 m robot keeping 2 cm clearance cannot pass. It now
   places it 15-22 % along the controller's own path, on the open first leg. The gap case needs global
   replanning (limitation, section 8).

3. LQR DESIGN ON THE MODEL (test_control.py Part 1)
====================================================================================================
  v [m/s]   K (e_y, e_theta)   closed-loop |z|     time constants [s]   damping
  0.04      (9.28, 2.79)       (0.993, 0.867)      (7.15, 0.35)         1.0
  0.08      (9.25, 2.92)       (0.986, 0.867)      (3.56, 0.35)         1.0
  0.15      (9.20, 3.11)       (0.974, 0.869)      (1.88, 0.36)         1.0

- Stable at every speed (|z| < 1); both poles real (no overshoot).
- The slow mode is the cross-track one and its time constant scales with 1/v: the robot can only correct a
  lateral error by driving (the nonholonomic constraint, Week 5 D1). The fast mode (heading, ~0.35 s) barely
  depends on speed.
- Both are well above the wheels' 0.11 s lag, which the LQR model leaves out.

4. TRADE-OFF CURVE AND THE NEGLECTED WHEEL LAG (Part 2)
====================================================================================================
v = 0.15 m/s, starting 100 mm off the path. "Nonlinear + lag" = the unicycle model with a first-order 0.11 s lag on
the turn rate.
  R scale   state cost   input cost   peak omega    settle to 5 mm   nonlinear + lag: settle   overshoot
  0.01      157.1        111.04       5.07 rad/s    5.80 s           5.95 s                    0.0 mm
  0.1       159.5         40.71       2.49 rad/s    5.85 s           5.95 s                    0.0 mm
  1         168.7         11.91       0.92 rad/s    6.05 s           6.10 s                    0.0 mm
  10        196.3          3.01       0.31 rad/s    6.55 s           6.55 s                    0.0 mm
  100       267.0          0.65       0.10 rad/s    8.75 s           8.65 s                    1.4 mm
- Defending Bryson's choice (R scale 1): making the controller 100x more aggressive (R x0.01) cuts settling only
  from 6.05 s to 5.80 s but multiplies the peak steering command by 5.5 (0.92 -> 5.07 rad/s, beyond the
  1.58 rad/s the wheels can deliver). Cross-track recovery is limited by forward speed, not steering effort.
- Neglecting the wheel lag is justified: every design settles within 0.15 s of the linear prediction in the
  nonlinear model with the lag.

Docking only, starting at the pre-dock pose (1.1, 1.0) moved sideways and/or rotated (seed 300):
  start offset              docked   time     distance   lateral   heading
  y   +0 mm, theta  +0 deg  yes      8.8 s     9 mm       0 mm      0.0 deg
  y +100 mm, theta  +0 deg  yes      8.8 s     6 mm      49 mm     -8.2 deg
  y -100 mm, theta  +0 deg  yes      8.8 s     6 mm     -49 mm      8.1 deg
  y +200 mm, theta  +0 deg  yes      9.1 s    -4 mm      95 mm    -16.0 deg
  y   +0 mm, theta +17 deg  yes      9.9 s     9 mm       1 mm     -0.1 deg
  y +150 mm, theta -17 deg  yes     10.0 s     2 mm      72 mm    -12.0 deg
- A start rotated on the normal docks perfectly: the bearing term turns the robot back.
- A start off the normal docks facing the marker but off-centre and skewed: about half the start offset remains
  laterally (49 mm of 100, 95 of 200) with ~8 deg of heading error per 100 mm. This is exactly the Week 9 D3
  prediction: the law only regulates range and bearing, so once the marker is centred and at d* both commands are
  zero, whatever the lateral offset. A unicycle cannot move sideways (nonholonomic), so removing the offset needs a
  manoeuvre the law never makes.
- Why the full mission still docks well: the LQR tracker, steering on the EKF estimate, delivers the robot to the
  pre-dock pose ON the normal (to within millimetres), already facing the dock on the spline: the "planned
  approach to a pre-dock point on the normal" that Week 9 suggests.
- Docking error is therefore set by how accurately the robot reaches the pre-dock pose: estimation error plus
  tracking error at that point. The dead-reckoning run (findings_C 4.2: 78 mm lateral) and the failures in
  findings_E 4.5 show the same effect.

8. LIMITATIONS AND NEXT STEPS
====================================================================================================
- Docking off the normal leaves a lateral error (section 6), and a push during docking fails 10/10 with every
  controller version (findings_E 4.6). Extension the tutorial itself suggests: a docking law that also uses the
  marker's yaw (polar-coordinate docking), or re-planning to the normal when the lateral offset at pre-dock is too
  large. The yaw from PnP is unreliable face on (planar ambiguity, findings_A_B 2.2), so a polar law would need to
  use it only close up.
- Docking stops ~10 mm short every time: it stops as soon as the camera reads |z_M - d*| < 10 mm, approaching
  from outside. A tighter DOCK_TOL trades this against docking time (the approach slows exponentially near d*,
  time constant 1 / lam_v = 2 s).
- The route is fixed waypoints chosen around the KNOWN obstacles; the lidar is used for safety and local detours,
  not global planning. An unmapped obstacle in a gap narrower than the robot plus clearance (e.g. between the
  yellow and green cylinders, 0.52 m) cannot be passed (2.4).
- Speed vs estimation: the faster spline mission gives the EKF fewer wall-marker updates (5.2). If robustness to
  bad odometry matters more than time, a lower nominal speed, or waiting at pre-dock until the EKF position sigma
  is small, would trade some of the 10 s back.
