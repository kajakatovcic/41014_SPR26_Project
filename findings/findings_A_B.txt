====================================================================================================
1. PLATFORM ISSUES FOUND IN THE SIMULATION MODEL
====================================================================================================

1.1 Chassis dragging on the floor (menagerie bug)
- The waffle_pi_base collision mesh includes the ball casters. Its convex hull touches the floor where the
  low-friction caster spheres do, but with friction 1.0. The chassis mesh carried 6.4 N of the robot's weight.
- MuJoCo uses the LARGER friction of two touching geoms, so even the "frictionless" caster spheres got the
  floor's friction of 1.0.
- Effect before the fix: commanded 0.15 m/s gave 0.071 m/s, and commanded 0.3 rad/s while driving gave 0.138 rad/s.
  The robot could not turn on the spot at all (0.001 rad/s for 0.5 commanded). The encoders overstated the motion
  (wheel slip), so dead reckoning was wrong too.
- Fix: give the casters and the chassis mesh the casters' contact properties, with a higher contact priority so
  they override the floor. After the fix the encoders match ground truth (0.117 against 0.116 m/s, 0.281 against
  0.279 rad/s).
- The same bug exists in the Burger model.

1.2 Wheel actuators: deadband and lag (see 4.2)
- Each wheel turns 1.0 rad/s slower than commanded, and not at all below 1 rad/s.
- Top speed is 0.227 m/s, not the 0.26 m/s the actuator limit suggests.

1.3 Simulation artefacts handled
- Markers flush with a surface z-fight with it and disappear at most angles. Fixed by mounting them 5 mm proud.
- The menagerie floor is reflective (reflectance 0.2). Reflections of markers were decoded as WRONG ids (id 17).
  This is realistic, since real floors reflect too, so it is handled in perception (2.2), not by changing the floor.
- MuJoCo 3.15: rangefinder sensors created from Python need intprm = [1, 0, 0] (distance output), otherwise the
  model fails to compile.

1.4 Sensor smoke test (outputs/setup_waffle_sensors.txt)
- 60 s headless: 2 wheel actuators, 360-beam lidar (0.12-3.5 m), 640 x 480 camera at 48.8 deg vertical FOV, all
  producing data (293/360 lidar hits at the start pose, camera frame mean grey level 91.5).

Defended deviations from the Week 4 pipeline (parameters follow the sensor, Week 4 5.2):
- Statistical outlier removal turned OFF. In Week 4 it removes RGB-D/stereo flying pixels; this lidar has constant
  noise and no flying points, and the filter deleted real sparse returns from distant obstacles.
  Waffle Pi, 300 poses: with filter 92.5% detected (94.7% within 2.5 m); without 96.9% (99.3%); 0 false clusters
  either way.
- DBSCAN eps = 0.10 m (tutorial 0.06) and min_points = 4 (tutorial 8). eps must exceed the beam spacing at max
  range (6 cm at 3.5 m) plus noise. A 16 cm cylinder 1.2-1.5 m away returns only 6-8 points.
  Comparison over 300 random poses (same scoring as tests/test_perception.py):
    settings                                                    detected   within 2.5 m   false clusters
    Week 4 lidar values: outlier removal (10 nb), eps 0.06, min 8   23.3%      24.5%          0
    Week 4 eps/min, outlier removal off                              22.4%      23.5%          0
    tuned eps/min, outlier removal on (5 nb)                         92.5%      94.7%          0
    tuned (module): outlier removal off, eps 0.10, min 4             96.9%      99.3%          0
  The first rows are scored with "visible = 4 beams", which min_points = 8 can never cluster. Scored fairly (visible
  = at least 8 beams, and 8 points needed on the object): Week 4 values 33.1% (34.6% within 2.5 m), tuned values
  90.9% (94.5%).
  Why the Week 4 values fail: DBSCAN only starts a cluster at a point with min_points neighbours within eps. On a
  single scan line with beam spacing s, a radius eps holds at most about 2 eps / s + 1 points: at 2 m
  (s = 3.5 cm) and eps = 6 cm that is ~4, so min_points = 8 is almost never reached. Week 4's lidar had 32 beam rows,
  so its points covered a surface and many more fell inside eps.
- RANSAC line instead of plane (2D scan). A line only counts as a wall if its inliers span >= 1 m (longer than any
  obstacle face), keeping only the longest unbroken run of points (gaps > 0.2 m split it). Without this, close box
  faces (40+ collinear points) were removed as "walls", and lines picked up points where other surfaces cross them.

ICP lidar odometry (Week 4 Bonus; Waffle Pi, unicycle circle v = 0.15, omega = 0.3, 105 scan pairs, 2.43 m):
- Point-to-plane ICP: per-step error 1.5 mm, 0.03 deg. Final drift 12 mm (0.5% of path) and 0.28 deg.
- Encoder dead reckoning over the same path: 13 mm, 1.70 deg. ICP is ~6x better in heading.
- Point-to-point against point-to-plane, same drive (2.45 m, 105 scan pairs):
    method                  max correspondence   per-step error           final drift
    point-to-point          0.1 m                3.0 mm, 0.165 deg        49 mm (2.0%), 2.08 deg
    point-to-point          0.3 m                3.0 mm, 0.161 deg        38 mm (1.6%), 3.44 deg
    point-to-plane          0.3 m                1.4 mm, 0.029 deg        10 mm (0.4%), 0.49 deg
  Point-to-point matches each beam to the nearest previous beam, which pulls the estimated motion towards zero;
  its yaw error per step is ~6x larger. The tutorial's point-to-plane is the right choice. (The final drift differs
  slightly from the test run above because the noise sequence differs.)
- A 2D scan has no defined surface normals (every neighbourhood is a line), so the scan is extruded into short
  vertical walls (+0.1 m) before normal estimation.

2.2 CAMERA + ArUco (640x480, vertical FOV 48.8 deg, 4x4_50 markers, 150 mm, centres at 0.13 m)

Detection (calibrated camera, 500 poses per seed; seed 0 rerun in outputs/perception.txt):
- 95-100% of fully visible markers detected within 2 m at viewing angles under 60 deg (100% under 30 deg).
- 2-3 m: 92-98% under 60 deg. 3-4 m: 82-98%. Above 60 deg off square-on: 57-78% at 1-2 m, 48-59% at 2-3 m.
- No wrong ids after map checking (0 in the rerun). Detections are rejected if the id is not in the map, or the
  marker centre is not at its mapped height (+/- 0.1 m). This removes floor reflections, which appear below the
  floor.

Range bias and its correction:
- Raw range is biased long, by an amount that grows with range squared.
  Raw mean +41.0 to +50.9 mm, RMS 60.9-72.8 mm (seeds 0-4; seed 0 rerun +45.8 mm, RMS 64.2 mm).
- Explanation (Week 3 wrap-up): range comes from apparent size n = f s / Z. Corners detected delta px inside the
  marker outline give a range error of Z^2 delta / (f s).
- Fitted corner bias: 0.60-0.68 px per seed (calibrated camera; seed 0 rerun 0.613 px). Module uses BIAS_PX = 0.64.
- After correction: range error mean -1.9 to -3.1 mm (each seed's own fit) / -4.6 to +1.0 mm (module values,
  seeds 0-5), RMS 29.9-35.4 mm. Seed 0 rerun: -2.2 mm own fit, -4.3 mm module values, RMS 29.9 mm.
- Tried and rejected: cornerSubPix refinement + re-solving PnP made far ranges unstable (up to -540 mm at 3.5 m).

Noise model handed to the EKF (Week 3 wrap-up):
- range sigma = Z^2 sigma_range_px / (f s), so it grows with range squared. Fitted 0.40-0.45 px (one seed 0.73);
  module uses 0.45 px.
- bearing sigma = sigma_bearing_px / f, constant. Fitted 0.21-0.25 px; module uses 0.24 px. Bearing error std is
  about 0.023 deg.
- Range scatter is larger than bearing scatter because range comes from apparent size and the PnP solution, both of
  which degrade at oblique views, while bearing comes only from where the centre lands in the image.
- Calibration term added to bearing: principal point uncertainty 0.2 px (see 2.3).
- Consistency check: errors inside the reported 2-sigma bounds, range 96-97%, bearing 97-99% (seeds 0-5, seed 5
  not used for fitting; seed 0 rerun 97 % / 99 %). Expect about 95%. Slightly conservative, as Week 7 recommends
  ("inflate Q_d").

Other observations:
- Planar pose ambiguity (Week 3 Part E): a marker seen nearly face on gave roll/pitch about 9 deg off while its
  position was within 1 cm. The EKF uses marker POSITION only (range, bearing). Docking should not rely on the
  marker's orientation.
- Measurement is range and bearing from the robot origin, through the camera mount T_RC, matching Week 7 h_rb.

2.3 CAMERA CALIBRATION (Week 3 Parts B, C; outputs/calibration.txt, figures/calibration.png)

Set-up: 9 x 6 internal corners, 40 mm squares, held in front of the stationary robot at 0.45-1.0 m with tilts up to
~35 deg, images through the noisy simulated camera.

Single calibration, 30 views (all 30 used):
- Pinhole model: f/rho_u = 529.71 px (+0.120%), f/rho_v = 529.88 px (+0.152%), true 529.08.
  u0 = 319.32 (-0.18 px), v0 = 239.22 (-0.28 px), true (319.5, 239.5).
- Reprojection RMS 0.111 px. Per image 0.039-0.388 px; worst is a steep, distant view.
- Full 5-term distortion model (Week 3 default, images2C): same RMS 0.111 px, so the extra terms explain nothing.
  Fitted (k1, k2, p1, p2, k3) = (0.008, -0.099, -0.0004, -0.0004, 0.388), which "distorts" pixels by up to 17.7 px
  in the image corners where the board never was. Principal point pulled to (-0.71, -0.96) px.
- The rerun reproduces the values in src/config.py exactly (CAMERA_CAL_F = (529.71, 529.88),
  CAMERA_CAL_PP = (319.32, 239.22)).

Repeatability (5 independent calibrations each):

  10     full distortion   +0.031 +/- 0.050   +0.350 +/- 0.700   +0.131 +/- 0.496   13.0 +/- 11.2
  10     pinhole           +0.002 +/- 0.051   -0.007 +/- 0.134   +0.054 +/- 0.217   0

- Decision: pinhole model. The simulated lens has no distortion, the data does not support the extra terms, and they
  make the principal point 5-10x less repeatable.
- Assumed principal-point convention ((W-1)/2, (H-1)/2) confirmed by the pinhole fits.

Camera mount from the calibration poses (frame management):
- Camera position in the robot frame: mean error (-1.1, +0.3, -0.2) mm, per-view scatter 1.0 mm.
- Orientation: mean error 0.07 deg, worst view 0.40 deg. Confirms T_RC in config.

How calibration error carries into perception:
- f error +0.12%: every range scaled by the same amount, +1.2 mm at 1 m. Negligible.
- u0 error 0.18 px: every bearing shifted by 0.18 / 529.7 rad = 0.0195 deg. Measured bearing bias -0.015 to
  -0.017 deg, matching. This is as large as the bearing noise itself (0.023 deg).
- With the noise model alone, bearing 2-sigma coverage dropped from 96-98% to 89-95%. On a real robot the bias
  cannot be measured or removed, so the principal point's calibration uncertainty (0.2 px) was added to the
  bearing variance. Coverage back to 97-99%.
- How much calibration matters for the whole system: findings_E 4.2 (a 2 % focal-length error is the most damaging
  sensor fault tested).

                                   heading within 2 deg   median position error   worst                prior 2-sigma
  marker yaw from PnP (Week 3 E)   89% of views           10 mm                   280 mm / 10.7 deg    100% inside
  marker yaw from lidar wall line  100% of views          3 mm                    17 mm / 0.2 deg      100% inside
- The PnP failures are the planar pose ambiguity (Week 3 E): FACE ON, -5.8 deg at 0.6 m and -10.7 deg at 1.5 m,

4. MODELLING (CRITERION A; outputs/model.txt, figures/model.png)
====================================================================================================

4.1 Platform (Waffle Pi)
- Wheel radius r = 0.033 m, track b = 0.288 m, actuator limit 7.88 rad/s, encoders 4096 ticks/rev.
- v = r (w_r + w_l) / 2,  omega = r (w_r - w_l) / b.
- Commands that would exceed the usable wheel speed are scaled down together (keeps the path curvature).

4.2 Wheel actuators (velocity servo torque = kv (w_cmd - w), joint Coulomb friction, armature)
- Deadband derived: frictionloss / kv = 0.1 / 0.1 = 1.0 rad/s. Measured 1.001-1.016 (mean 1.008) rad/s.
  Below a 1 rad/s command the wheel does not move.
- Time constant derived: J / kv with J = armature 0.01 + wheel 2.1e-5 + m r^2 / 2 (m = 1.857 kg) = 0.0110 kg m^2,
  so 0.110 s. Measured 0.108 s.
- Turning resistance (identified, not derived): a constant 0.020 +/- 0.001 rad/s shortfall in turn rate at every
  turn rate tested (0.2-1.4 rad/s, spinning or arcing). Coulomb-like, most likely tyre scrub across the tread; the
  encoders see it too.
- Usable wheel speed 6.88 rad/s: top speed 0.227 m/s, top turn rate on the spot 1.58 rad/s.
- Compensation in unicycle_to_wheels(): +1.0 rad/s per wheel and +0.02 rad/s turn rate, in the direction of motion.

4.3 Models
- Unicycle: x = (x, y, theta), u = (v, omega). Continuous (Week 5 D) and exact-arc discrete with Jacobian F
  (Week 7 C.4).
- Tracking error in the reference frame, e = (e_x, e_y, e_theta), reference moving at (v_r, omega_r):
    e_x' = v cos(e_theta) - v_r + omega_r e_y
    e_y' = v sin(e_theta) - omega_r e_x
    e_theta' = omega - omega_r
  Linearised:  A = [[0, omega_r, 0], [-omega_r, 0, v_r], [0, 0, 0]],  B = [[1, 0], [0, 0], [0, 1]],
  du = (v - v_r, omega - omega_r).
  Verified: Jacobians match sympy; the error kinematics match the simulated unicycle; F matches a numerical
  Jacobian to 1.4e-10.
- 2-state model (Weeks 5 D, 8 B): A = [[0, v_r], [0, 0]], B = [[0], [1]]. It is the (e_y, e_theta) block when
  speed is not controlled. Sign convention: e = actual - reference (Week 8 writes theta_d - theta). This is the
  model the LQR tracker uses (findings_D); the omega_r term enters as the curvature feed-forward.

4.5 Observability of the pose (x, y, theta) (Week 5 C4, Week 7 C/D)
Method: local observability of the linearised time-varying system over 10 camera frames (1 s),
O = [W H_0; W H_1 F_0; ...], weighted by the perception noise model (W = R^-1/2), so singular values are in
"standard deviations per unit of state error". Test pose (-1.4, 0.2, 270 deg) sees markers 1 and 3.
Moving = (0.15 m/s, 0.2 rad/s).
  sensor set                  still: rank, singular values      moving: rank, singular values
  encoders only               0  [0, 0, 0]                      0  [0, 0, 0]
  1 marker, range + bearing   2  [7136, 431, 0]                 2  [7513, 493, 0]
  1 marker, range only        1  [431, 0, 0]                    2  [479, 17, 0]
  1 marker, bearing only      1  [7136, 0, 0]                   2  [7513, 116, 0]
  1 marker, then another      3  [6285, 1739, 244]              3  [6474, 1839, 255]
  2 markers at once           3  [8889, 2459, 346]              3  [9254, 2636, 386]
- Encoders are inputs to the motion model, not measurements of the pose: they observe nothing, so dead reckoning
  drifts without bound.
- KEY RESULT: one marker NEVER makes the full pose observable, even while moving. Rotating the robot AND its whole
  path about the marker changes no range or bearing measurement, so one direction stays unobservable. Contrast
  Week 8, where heading became observable through motion with GPS position fixes.
- Two markers, at the same time or one after the other with odometry linking them, make the full pose observable.
  This is why the wall landmarks are needed, not just the dock marker.
- Bearing carries far more information than range (singular values ~7000 against ~430 for the same marker).
- This result shows up again in the system trials: the pre-dock pose sees essentially only the dock marker, so an
  estimate that arrives there wrong cannot be fully corrected (findings_E 4.5).

Room coverage (0.1 m grid x 24 headings, free space only, marker usable if in FOV, within 3 m, viewed under
60 deg off square-on and not occluded): 22,848 poses
- 60% see no marker (dead reckoning only)
- 38% see one marker (corrects 2 of 3 directions)
- 2% see two or more (corrects the full pose at once)
- Implication: the EKF mostly gets corrections from markers seen one after another, with drift growing between.
  Possible design extension: more or better-placed markers (quantifiable with the same map).

4.6 Discretisation at T = 0.05 s (Week 5 C5)
- 2-state, v_r = 0.15: ZOH B_d = [0.000187, 0.05]^T; Euler B_d = [0, 0.05]^T, missing the T^2/2 v_r term.
- 3-state, v_r = 0.15, omega_r = 0.4: ZOH keeps the open-loop eigenvalues on the unit circle (max |z| = 1.000000);
  forward Euler pushes them outside (1.000200), an artificial instability for turning references.
- T = 0.05 s is under half the wheel time constant (0.11 s) and twice the camera rate.
- (Anthony Fava's 3-state LQRTracker used forward Euler; this is one of the reasons it was not merged, findings_D 2.)

4.7 Does the robot agree? (Week 5 D2)
Command sequence (v, omega) for (s): (0.15, 0) 3; (0.15, 0.4) 4; (0, 0.8) 3; (0.12, -0.3) 4; (0.2, 0) 2.
Path ~1.9 m, 16 s.
  wheel commands   prediction                   final position error   final heading error
  raw              ideal unicycle               396 mm                 43.1 deg
  raw              unicycle + actuator model      3 mm                  0.5 deg
  compensated      ideal unicycle                25 mm                  0.2 deg
  compensated      unicycle + actuator model      5 mm                  0.3 deg
- The actuator model (deadband + lag + turning resistance) explains the robot almost exactly.
- With compensated commands the plain unicycle is good to ~25 mm over 16 s (remaining error: the 0.11 s lag at
  command changes), so the controller can use the simple model.

4.8 Assumptions to state in the write-up
- Flat floor; no wheel slip in the longitudinal direction (holds after the caster fix: encoders = truth).
- Wheel actuators: deadband + first-order lag + constant turning resistance; lag neglected in the control model.
  Confirmed by the controller design: the closed-loop time constants are 0.35 s and 1.4-7 s against the 0.11 s lag,
  and every design settles within 0.15 s of the linear prediction with the lag included (findings_D 4).
- Encoders quantised at 4096 ticks/rev.
- Markers at known, fixed positions (the map); camera rigidly mounted (T_RC).
- Lidar and camera noise as in simulation: lidar 1 cm constant; camera Gaussian pixel noise of 2 grey levels.

5. OPEN ITEMS / LIMITATIONS
====================================================================================================
- Lidar detection falls to 63% at 2.5-3 m and 0% beyond 3 m; the dock drops to 83% at 2-2.5 m. The camera covers
  the dock.
- Marker detection drops above 60 deg viewing angle; marker orientation unreliable face on.
- The noise model is fitted to simulation; hardware would need its own calibration and noise characterisation.
- The marker visibility map treats a marker as usable within 3 m and 60 deg; refine with the measured detection
  rates if needed.
- Observability analysis is local (linearised); the room map uses geometry only, not the EKF itself.
