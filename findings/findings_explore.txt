====================================================================================================
1. WHY: THE REQUIREMENT
====================================================================================================
To port to hardware easily, the robot must start with a fresh view of the environment:
  - no map of the room, walls or obstacles, and no preplanned route;
  - no known start pose: it localises from an ArUco marker it can see, and that marker's frame becomes its
    reference frame;
  - it finds the docking station from the known offset between that marker and the dock marker;
  - it avoids obstacles with what the lidar sees in real time, still using the spline trajectory generation.
What the robot is given (src/config.py), and nothing else:
  REF_MARKER_ID = 1                  the marker it localises from
  DOCK_OFFSET = (3.790, 1.800, 180)  the dock marker's pose in the reference marker's frame (m, m, deg); on hardware
                                     measured with a tape; checked against the simulated room (test_navigation Part 0)
  marker size, mounting height (0.13 m), dictionary, camera calibration, robot geometry (as before)


====================================================================================================
2. DESIGN
====================================================================================================

2.1 Frames
- {A}: the reference marker's floor frame. Origin at the marker centre, x along its normal (out of its face, into
  the room), y to the left. The estimator, the lidar map and every planned path are in {A}.
- Evaluation only: estimates are converted to the world frame through the reference marker's true pose, to compare
  with ground truth (main.marker_frame, pose_to_world).

2.2 Mission modes (src/control/navigation.py Navigator, built on MissionController)
  search    turn on the spot (0.7 rad/s) until the reference marker is in view
  localise  stand still 1 s (10 camera frames) while the estimator initialises (2.3)
  look      turn to where the dock marker should be and look for up to 1.5 s: one marker leaves the rotation about it
            unobservable (findings_A_B 4.5), a second known marker fixes the full pose
  plan      plan from the estimate to the pre-dock pose (0.75 m out on the dock marker's normal) on the lidar
            occupancy grid (2.4)
  turn/track/dock   as the route mission: Lyapunov turn on the spot (Week 6 B.6), LQR path following with the
            curvature feed-forward (Weeks 5, 6, 8; Bryson gains, findings_D 3-4), Week 9 D3 docking
  find      the dock marker has not been in view for 3 s at the pre-dock pose (the estimate has drifted): turn on the
            spot until it is seen, hold 1 s while the EKF corrects, replan
  back off  while docking, the estimate puts the robot more than 4 cm off the dock marker's normal (a push): reverse
            straight out to 1.15 m from the dock marker (lidar check behind), replan to the pre-dock pose and dock
            again; at most 3 times. The Week 9 law cannot remove a sideways offset itself (findings_D 6)
- Replanning (every lidar scan, 5 Hz): when the path ahead (1.5 m) runs within the robot's radius of a lidar return
  AND closer than when it was planned (something new); when the estimate is > 0.15 m from the path (a correction);
  when the lidar safety corridor stops the robot for 0.5 s.
- A correction that moves the estimate more than the robot could drive in one period (> 5 cm) restarts the lidar
  map, since it was drawn from a wrong pose.

2.3 Estimation (src/estimation/estimation.py MarkerSLAM; extends the Week 7 C.4 EKF)
- Initialisation (Week 3 E + Week 4): position from the marker's PnP pose (range and bearing are accurate); the
  marker's YAW (which way it faces) from the lidar's RANSAC wall line it hangs on, since the PnP yaw suffers the
  planar pose ambiguity (Part 1 of test_navigation: up to 10.7 deg wrong face on). Falls back to the PnP yaw if no
  wall passes through the marker. Prior covariance = Jacobian of the pose with respect to (range, bearing, yaw) with
  the measurement noise and sigma_yaw = 1 deg (wall) or 6 deg (PnP): the yaw uncertainty appears as a rotation about
  the marker, the direction one marker cannot observe.
- Known landmarks: the reference marker at the origin and the dock marker at DOCK_OFFSET; Week 7 range-and-bearing
  updates with the same Q/R tuning, gate and lock-out recovery as the route EKF.
- Wall-line heading update (extension): when a lidar wall line from the same moment passes within 0.15 m of a known
  marker in view and agrees with its PnP normal to 30 deg, the wall's normal in the robot frame is the marker's
  facing direction psi; its direction phi_M in {A} is known, so z = psi = phi_M - theta, H = [0, 0, -1], sigma 1 deg,
  gated (chi-square 1 dof, 8.81). One marker plus its wall observes the FULL pose; one marker alone cannot (Part A,
  findings_A_B 4.5). Repeated rejections (5 in a row, a turn the odometry did not see) trigger the same lock-out
  recovery as the marker measurements. The main loop reads the lidar before the camera when both are due, so the
  wall line and the marker are from the same instant.
- EKF-SLAM (Week 7 C "where this goes next"): any other marker is added to the state when first seen (inverse
  range-and-bearing model with its Jacobians), but only once the dock marker has been seen (anchored). Markers mapped
  before that are placed from an estimate whose rotation is not yet observed, and later pull it away from the dock
  marker (found in testing, 3.3). Mapped markers are dropped when the lock-out recovery fires.
- Dead reckoning for comparison starts from the same initial estimate.

2.4 Planning (src/control/planning.py; NOT from the tutorials, a minimal extension)
- Log-odds occupancy grid in {A}, 5 cm cells: each lidar return marks its cell occupied (+0.85), the cells along
  the beam free (-0.4), limits -2 / +3.5, occupied above 0.5. Placed with the estimated pose.
- A* (8-connected): cells within robot radius + 5 cm (0.30 m) of an occupied cell are blocked; cells within 0.6 m
  cost up to 5x more, so paths keep to open space. Unknown cells count as free (optimistic): the robot plans straight
  at the goal and replans as it sees more.
- A* plans to an approach point 0.3 m back along the dock marker's normal from the pre-dock pose; the last 0.3 m is
  a straight run along the normal, so the robot arrives lined up for docking.
- The A* path is shortened to a few waypoints by line-of-sight (string pulling), then splined with control.Path
  (Anthony Fava's Trajectory geometry) ending at the dock heading, starting along the robot's heading if that is
  within 30 deg of the path (no stop to turn), otherwise free (turn on the spot first). The spline must keep the
  robot's radius + 3 cm from every return and be no tighter than curvature 15 /m (the controller keeps
  v kappa <= 0.8 rad/s, so it is still driven at >= 0.053 m/s; a U-turn squeezed into a few cm would be crawled at
  ~0); otherwise fewer shortcuts are taken (more waypoints), and straight legs with turns on the spot are the last
  resort.
- The controller follows it exactly as the route: LQR on cross-track/heading with the curvature feed-forward.


====================================================================================================
3. DEVELOPMENT: WHAT TESTING CHANGED
====================================================================================================
3.1 Initial heading from PnP alone (the first version)
- Default start, marker seen face on at 0.75 m: initial error 91 mm / 6.9 deg, every one of 10 frames identical (the
  ambiguity is in the geometry, not noise). EKF RMS 49-105 mm over the mission, max up to 281 mm, until the dock
  marker was seen; 10/10 robustness nominal trials still docked, but one random start flipped to a 1.8 m error.
- Fix: the marker yaw from the lidar wall line (2.3). Default start: 2 mm / 0.1 deg; mission EKF RMS 9 mm.
3.2 Grid drawn from a wrong pose: walls mapped while the estimate was 7 deg out stayed as ghost walls after the
  correction -> the map restarts on large corrections (2.2).
3.3 Lost robot with an obstacle that appeared (straight segments, popup at 10 s): heading drifted to -16 deg on a
  long detour with no known marker in view; at the "pre-dock pose" the dock marker was outside the camera's view and
  the robot waited until timeout. Fix 1: find mode (2.2). It then found the dock but docked 145 mm off: markers 2 and
  4 had been mapped ~1 m out while lost and outvoted the dock marker (32 dock measurements gated out). Fix 2: map
  only after anchoring; drop mapped markers on recovery (2.3). After both: docked, 3 mm lateral.
3.4 Replanning on every scan: a goal next to an obstacle (the popup dropped 0.27 m from the pre-dock pose) or a start
  inside the margin made every new path "blocked" at birth -> 598 replans. Fix: replan only when the path is now
  closer to a return than when planned (2.2).
3.5 A* returning a one- or two-cell path crashed the waypoint step (empty array shape) -> fixed.
3.6 First full robustness run (all of the above, before 3.7): 104/130. Every scenario that does not move the robot
  behind its odometry's back was 10/10 or 9/10 (one obstacle_appears timeout), but:
    odometry_x3 7/10, push 4/10, combined 4/10: all "docked off target, EKF 63-335 mm off at the pre-dock pose";
    push_docking 0/10 (61 mm lateral, as in every route-mission version).
  Cause: after the disturbance, the only marker in view near the dock is the dock marker, and one marker cannot
  observe the rotation about it (Part A), so the estimate arrived at the pre-dock pose wrong and the Week 9 law
  docked off the normal.
3.7 Fixes: the wall-line heading update (2.3) and back off (2.2). The first version of back off replanned straight
  from 0.3 m in front of the dock to the pre-dock pose facing the dock: a U-turn in 0.3 m, which the curvature speed
  limit crawled at 1 mm/s until timeout (9/10 push_docking timeouts); fixed by reversing out first and by the
  planner's curvature limit (2.4). On the same seeds: odometry_x3 7 -> 9/10, combined 4 -> 9/10 (before the
  back-off fix), push 4 -> 10/10, push_docking 0 -> 10/10.
3.8 The first curvature limit (5 /m) quietly turned the spline off: test_navigation's spline and straight rows came
  out identical in 9 of 10 missions, because nearly every spline was rejected and the planner fell back to straight
  legs. Logging each candidate showed hooks at the END (up to 57 /m: the last string-pulled waypoint sat just short of
  the pre-dock pose at an angle, and the spline had to arrive at the dock heading) and once at the START (52 /m:
  starting along the robot's heading towards a close first waypoint). Fixes: the straight approach along the normal,
  a free-start retry, and the limit at 15 /m. Splines were then used in 10/10 missions, docking at 0-2 mm lateral
  (before: up to 17 mm, arriving at an angle).

4.4 Estimation (test_estimation, 5 missions from the default start, seeds 100-104)
  EKF RMS 11.3 mm (5.2-22.1 per mission), worst 69 mm; dead reckoning from the same start estimate 86 mm RMS,
  up to 232 mm at the end.
  Consistency: mean NEES 1.88 (3 = consistent; below = conservative), NEES under the 97.5 % bound on 99 % of 4579
  steps, 2-sigma coverage 100 % / 100 %, mean NIS 0.55, 0 gate rejections. Verdict: consistent, on the
  conservative side.
  Wall-line heading updates: 74 in a traced mission, none rejected, mean NIS 0.02: the 1 deg sigma is conservative
  (the wall fit is ~0.1 deg), as Week 7 advises when unsure.
  Tuning replay (Week 7 D.4): Q x3 (as the route EKF) gives NEES 1.88; Q x1 4.04 (slightly overconfident); Q x0.1
  catastrophic (66 mm, 333 good measurements rejected). R scales 1-10 change accuracy by < 1.2 mm. The route
  mission's tuning carries over.
  Where the error comes from: the worst mission (trial 0, 69 mm) drove 6-21 s with no known marker in view (the
  dock marker was not detected during "look" from 3.5 m) and its heading drifted 3 deg on odometry; the first dock
  marker sighting corrected it to 0.1 deg. Accuracy is set by how long the robot drives without a known marker.
  Heading RMS 0.75 deg against 0.1 deg in the route mission (five known markers): fewer known landmarks.

====================================================================================================
5. LIMITATIONS AND NEXT STEPS
====================================================================================================
- The wall-line yaw needs the marker on a straight wall at least 1 m long (the RANSAC wall threshold) in the
  lidar's view. A free-standing marker falls back to PnP's yaw (up to 10.7 deg wrong face on, 4.1).
- The reference marker must be in view (or found by turning) at the start, and DOCK_OFFSET must be measured
  accurately: an error in it moves the pre-dock pose by the same amount (the docking law still centres the robot
  on the dock marker, findings_D 6).
- Planner: a minimal extension, not from the tutorials. 5 cm grid, circular robot (0.25 m), full A* replans
  (~0.1 s), unknown space treated as free. Not tested: moving obstacles, gaps under 0.6 m, mazes, rooms larger than
  the fixed grid (9 m x 9 m in {A}).
- An obstacle dropped right beside the pre-dock approach (0.27 m from the pre-dock pose, development test) made the
  robot stop and replan ~100 times before it got through and docked (22 mm lateral): the goal itself is inside the
  planner's margin. A goal that moves away from obstacles would handle it.
- Estimation accuracy depends on how long the robot drives with no known marker in view (4.4). Mapped markers
  (EKF-SLAM) only help after the dock marker anchors the estimate.
