FINDINGS: INTEGRATION AND ROBUSTNESS (CRITERION E) - ROUTE MISSION
TurtleBot3 Waffle Pi, MuJoCo, Track A
This file is the ROUTE mission (now tests/test_robustness.py --mission route; numbers unchanged). The default
explore mission's 13 scenarios (130/130 successful) and the comparison with these results are in
findings_explore.txt sections 4.5-4.6.
Raw notes, to be formatted for the project page / report. Companion to findings_A_B.txt, findings_C_loop.txt
and findings_D_control.txt.

Where the numbers come from (raw output in outputs/, figures in figures/):
  python tests/test_robustness.py --trials 10                       merged spline controller   outputs/robustness_lqr.txt
                                                                                               figures/robustness_lqr.png
  python tests/test_robustness.py --trials 10 --controller straight straight segments          outputs/robustness_straight.txt
                                                                                               figures/robustness_straight.png
120 missions per controller (12 scenarios x 10 trials), ~7 min (spline) / ~10 min (straight) on 16 cores. For
these outputs the scenarios were run in two batches of six and the results merged (same seeds as a single run).
Seeds: scenario k, trial i uses seed 1000 k + i, so every trial is repeatable and both controllers see the same
disturbances.


====================================================================================================
1. METHOD
====================================================================================================
- Every trial is the full system: perception -> EKF -> LQR tracking -> visual docking, on the mission in
  src/config.py (START_POSE -> spline route -> pre-dock pose -> dock).
- Scenarios change the WORLD only (main.run keyword arguments), never the robot's software or its map. Each
  scenario draws its disturbance at random per trial, on top of random wheel-radius errors (up to +/-0.8 % per
  wheel) that every trial has.
- Success = docked (by the robot's own docking test), within 30 mm (distance and lateral) and 5 deg of the target
  docked pose (ground truth), no collision with anything but the floor, under 150 s.
- Collisions: every MuJoCo contact between the robot's bodies and any geom other than the floor is recorded.
- Failure diagnosis, automatic from the logged data (tests/test_robustness.py diagnose()):
    collision                                -> controller / route
    not docked, EKF > 100 mm off             -> estimator lost
    not docked, stopped by the lidar         -> blocked, no detour found (controller)
    not docked, in dock mode                 -> docking did not converge (perception / docking law)
    docked off target, pushed while docking  -> docking law + one-marker sensing layout
    docked off target, EKF > 30 mm off at the pre-dock pose -> estimator
    docked off target, good estimate         -> perception / docking law

Scenarios:
  nominal              wheel radius errors up to +/-0.8 %, default noise
  start_small          robot placed up to 0.1 m and 5 deg from where its software thinks (prior: 5 cm, 3 deg)
  start_large          up to 0.3 m and 30 deg off
  odometry_x3          wheel radius errors up to +/-2.5 %, 3x slip
  camera_noise_x4      camera pixel noise 8 grey levels (4x)
  calibration_error    the real camera's field of view 1 deg off the calibrated one (focal length ~2 % off)
  lidar_noise_x3       lidar range noise 3 cm (3x)
  landmarks_missing    wall markers 2 and 4 missing from the room (still in the robot's map)
  push                 shoved sideways, 15-20 N for 0.2 s, either side, at a random time in 10-25 s, while driving
                       the route (on this robot 15 N moves it ~5 cm and turns it ~11 deg, 17 N ~9 cm / ~18 deg)
  push_docking         the same shove 2-5 s after visual docking starts
  unexpected_obstacle  a cylinder (radius 8-12 cm) not in the map, on the controller's own planned path 15-22 % of
                       the way along (the open first leg), up to 5 cm either side of it
  combined             start up to 0.1 m / 5 deg off + 3x odometry errors + 2x camera noise + a push on the route
Changes since the first version of these trials: the push window moved from 10-40 s to 10-25 s so that it always
lands on the route (the spline reaches the pre-dock pose at ~30 s), and pushing during docking became its own
scenario (push_docking), so the two failure mechanisms are measured separately.


====================================================================================================
2. ROBUSTNESS MECHANISMS ADDED BECAUSE OF THESE TRIALS (the failures that drove them)
====================================================================================================

2.1 EKF lock-out recovery (src/estimation/estimation.py)
- First trials: a 17 N push and a 0.2 m / 15 deg start error BOTH failed (not docked, EKF 576 mm and 1166 mm off),
  with 1138 and 904 marker measurements rejected by the chi-square gate.
- Cause (estimator): after a jump the odometry cannot see, P is still small and confident, so every CORRECT
  marker measurement fails the gate as an "outlier" and the filter never corrects itself. The gate (added in C to
  reject outliers) locks the filter out.
- Fix: if every marker measurement in a camera frame has been rejected for 5 frames in a row (0.5 s), the filter
  concludes it is the one that is wrong, adds diag(0.3 m, 0.3 m, 30 deg)^2 to P, and reprocesses the frame.
- After the fix, single runs: push 17 N docked (EKF peak 90 mm, back to 0 mm); push 20 N docked (193 mm -> 1 mm);
  start 0.2 m / 15 deg docked (184 mm -> 0 mm); start 0.3 m / 30 deg docked (296 mm -> 1 mm); one recovery each.
- Final run: 0 recoveries in every nominal, camera-noise, lidar-noise, calibration, landmark and obstacle trial
  (no false triggers); 0.9-2.2 per mission where the robot is moved or the odometry is bad.

2.2 Lidar detour planning (src/control/control.py, MissionController._plan_detour; findings_D 1.5)
- Before: the lidar safety stop was permanent, so an unexpected obstacle on the route meant waiting until
  timeout.
- Fix: if the path stays blocked for 1 s, plan a sidestep from the lidar returns (two waypoints beside the
  obstacle, on the side with more clearance, every new leg checked against every return), then rejoin the planned
  path. Not from the tutorials: a minimal local replanner, justified by the brief ("navigate using the LiDAR").
- Bugs found by the trials and fixed:
    1. the obstacle's far edge was taken from every return in a 1.5 m window, which included the dock; the second
       leg then ended 0.21 m from the dock and the check (rightly) rejected it. Fixed by measuring only the
       blocking obstacle.
    2. the clearance check included the robot's current position, which is inside the margin because it has just
       stopped ~0.25 m from the obstacle, so the first plan was always rejected (3/10 obstacle trials failed).
       Fixed: the first, sideways leg only has to not get closer than the robot already is.
    3. (with the spline, findings_D 2.4) rejoining through the remaining waypoints with straight legs cut into
       known obstacles; and overshooting a ~92 deg detour corner left the robot driving straight on. Fixed by
       rejoining the spline itself and by corner detection.

====================================================================================================
4. INTERPRETATION (failures traced to causes)
====================================================================================================

4.1 What the system is NOT sensitive to
- Camera noise x4 and lidar noise x3: indistinguishable from nominal (EKF 3.0 / 3.4 mm against 3.1 mm, docking
  unchanged). Random noise is averaged away by the filter (EKF) and by DBSCAN/RANSAC (lidar).
- Small start errors (within ~2x the prior): absorbed without recovery events.

4.2 Calibration error is the most damaging SENSOR fault (perception)
- A 1 deg field-of-view error (~2 % focal length) gives NEES 165 (badly overconfident) and EKF RMS 28 mm against
  3 mm nominal, though every trial still succeeds (5.4 mm lateral, 0.9 deg).
- Why: a focal-length error is a SYSTEMATIC scale error on every range (Week 3: range from apparent size n = f s/Z)
  and the filter models only random noise, so it trusts biased measurements. 4x random noise did nothing.
- Links back to criterion B: calibration accuracy matters more than image noise; test_calibration.py's 0.12 %
  focal error is ~17x smaller than this scenario's.
- On the old route the same fault docked 13.8 mm to the side; on the new route 4.5-5.4 mm with either controller,
  so the improvement comes from the route. The exact mechanism was not isolated.

4.3 Odometry and start errors: covered by the estimator, with recoveries
- odometry_x3: EKF RMS 25 mm, worst 245 mm, NEES 45, 1.5 recoveries per mission: the Q_d tuned for +/-0.8 %
  wheel errors is too small for +/-2.5 %, so the filter becomes overconfident and the lock-out recovery has to step
  in. 9/10 docked accurately; the failure is 4.5.
- start_large: 0.9 recoveries per mission; NEES 9.2 from the initial transient; worst 328 mm; all succeed.
- The lock-out recovery is what makes these pass (section 2.1).

4.4 Missing landmarks: the Part A observability result in practice (estimator / sensing layout)
- With markers 2 and 4 gone, the EKF drifts to 66 mm worst case (RMS 9.6 mm), NEES stays 1.89 (honest: the
  covariance grows with the drift) and every trial docks well (1.5 mm lateral).
- Much better than on the old route (248 mm worst case, 37 mm RMS), with either controller (68 mm on straight
  segments). Likely reason: the new route's last ~1 m drives straight at the dock marker, so it anchors the
  estimate for longer (seed 0: 250 dock-marker detections against 154 on the old route). With 60 % of poses seeing no marker (findings_A_B 4.5), WHERE the route goes matters as much as the
  filter.

4.5 Off-target docking after the estimator falls behind (estimator + sensing layout + docking law)
- In all three spline failures the EKF was 110-135 mm off when docking began, so the robot reached the "pre-dock
  pose" off the dock marker's normal.
- From the pre-dock pose the camera sees essentially only the dock marker. One marker cannot remove a full pose
  error (Part A observability: rotation about the marker is unobservable), so the estimate cannot be fixed there.
- The Week 9 D3 docking law then nulls range and bearing only, leaving about half the start offset as a lateral
  error (findings_D 6: 49 mm per 100 mm). Result: docked 35-58 mm to the side, 6-10 deg skewed.
- Trace: disturbance (bad odometry, push) -> estimator not yet re-converged at pre-dock (sensing layout: one
  marker) -> docking law cannot remove a lateral offset (controller). No single module is "wrong"; the weakness is
  where they meet.
- Why the spline fails here 3 times and straight segments 0 times (same seeds):
    - Pre-dock EKF error, combined scenario: spline 110 and 135 mm in its two failures; straight segments at most
      87 mm in any trial. odometry_x3: one trial above 100 mm for each (129 mm spline -> fail; 131 mm straight ->
      exactly at the limit).
    - Time to recover: pushes land at 10-25 s. The spline reaches the pre-dock pose at ~30 s, the straight
      segments at ~40 s, so the filter has 5-20 s against 15-30 s to re-converge.
    - Marker information: the straight-segment mission collects 47 % more wall-marker updates (seed 0: 309
      against 210), from 10 s more driving and the camera sweeping the markers during each turn on the spot.
  At 10 trials per scenario a 3-trial difference is within trial-to-trial variation (a 9/10 rate has a 95 %
  interval of roughly 55-100 %), but the mechanism is consistent with the logged data. It is a measured trade-off
  of the merge: 10 s faster, 5.7x smoother and 2.3x tighter tracking (findings_D 5.1) for slightly less margin
  against bad odometry.
- Possible fixes, each a justified extension:
    a) wait at pre-dock until the EKF position sigma is small, and re-approach the normal if it is not (needs b)
    b) a landmark visible from the pre-dock pose (e.g. beside the dock), so the full pose is observable there
    c) a docking law that also uses the marker's yaw (polar-coordinate docking, suggested in Week 9 D3), using the
       marker's orientation only close up, where the planar ambiguity (findings_A_B 2.2) is small
    d) slow down over the last part of the route (trades back some of the 10 s)

4.6 Push during docking: 0/10 for every controller version (docking law + sensing layout)
- A 15-20 N shove 2-5 s into docking moves the robot ~5-9 cm sideways. The robot keeps docking (10/10 docked, by
  its own test) but ends 42-90 mm to the side (mean 61 mm) and 10 deg skewed.
- Same mechanism as 4.5 at its most direct: the docking law steers on range and bearing only, so it re-centres the
  marker in the image but cannot remove the sideways offset; and with only the dock marker in view the EKF cannot
  observe it either (NEES 244).
- Every version shares the Week 9 docking law, so this is the clearest argument for fix c) in 4.5 (or backing out
  to the pre-dock pose when a disturbance is detected during docking).

4.7 Pushes on the route and the combined scenario
- push: 10/10. EKF worst 299 mm, NEES 84 until the recovery fires (1.1 per mission); the filter re-converges
  before pre-dock in every trial (pre-dock EKF error <= 50 mm, 8 of 10 within 2 mm).
- combined: 8/10, mean lateral docking error 12.3 mm; EKF worst 457 mm with 2.2 recoveries per mission: the filter
  is lost more than once per mission and recovers each time. Its two failures are 4.5, not an interaction of faults.

4.8 Unexpected obstacle
- 10/10; one detour per trial; mission time 40 -> 52 s; estimation unaffected (3.6 mm); path RMS 3.9 mm against
  the replanned path (the robot follows its detour as accurately as the route).
- Placement limits: the scenario puts the obstacle on the open first leg. In the 0.52 m gap between the yellow
  and green cylinders an extra obstacle cannot be passed locally; that needs global replanning.


====================================================================================================
5. TRADE-OFFS QUANTIFIED (for the Distinction band)
====================================================================================================
- Speed and smoothness vs estimation margin (the merge): the spline saves 10 s and is 5.7x smoother than straight
  segments on the same route, at the cost of 3 of 120 trials under bad odometry or pushes (4.5).
- Consistency vs responsiveness: the chi-square gate protects against outliers (C), but after an unseen
  disturbance it locks the filter out (2.1). The recovery rule (5 frames, 0.3 m / 30 deg inflation) restores
  responsiveness. The price is visible in consistency: until the recovery fires, the filter is badly overconfident
  (mission-mean NEES 84 in push, 46 in combined, against 2.8 nominal).
- Q_d tuning vs odometry quality: Q x3 is consistent for +/-0.8 % wheel errors (NEES 2.8) but overconfident at
  +/-2.5 % (NEES 45). A larger Q would cover worse wheels but make the nominal filter more conservative
  (findings_C 4.1: Q x10 gives NEES 1.77).
- Detour safety margin vs feasibility: requiring robot radius + 2 cm on the new legs found a detour in 10/10
  trials; requiring the full margin from the robot's current position found none at first (2.2).
- Mission time: detours add ~12 s; lock-out recoveries add ~1-2 s (push 41 s, combined 42 s against 40 s).


====================================================================================================
6. LIMITATIONS
====================================================================================================
- The route is fixed waypoints chosen around the known obstacles; the lidar is used for safety and local detours,
  not for global planning (4.8).
- The detour planner handles one compact obstacle with open floor beside it; it has not been tested with clutter,
  or obstacles near corners or the dock.
- 10 trials per scenario: a 9/10 rate means the true rate is roughly 55-100 % (95 % binomial interval), so
  quote rates with their trial counts, and treat 1-3 trial differences between controllers as indicative.
- Disturbances are simulated (pushes as a 0.2 s force; odometry errors as radius scale + random slip); real slip
  on a real floor may differ.
- Not tested: camera latency, motion blur, lighting changes, dynamic (moving) obstacles, a wrong marker map.
