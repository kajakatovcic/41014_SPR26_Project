1. Method

  • Every trial is the whole system, from perception through the EKF, planner and LQR to visual docking (tests/test_robustness.py).
  • Scenarios change the world but not the robot's software. Each draws its disturbance at random per trial, on top of random wheel-radius errors of up to ±0.8% that every trial has. Scenario k, trial i uses seed 1000k + i so every trial is repeatable and every controller sees the same disturbances.
  • Success = docked by the robot's own test, within 30 mm (distance and lateral) and 5° of the true docked pose, with no collision with anything but the floor, in under 150 s. Every MuJoCo contact between the robot and any other geom is recorded.
  • Every failure is diagnosed automatically from the logged data - a collision, an estimator that is lost (EKF more than 100 mm off), a robot blocked with no detour, docking that did not converge, or docking off target with either a bad estimate at the pre-dock pose or a good one.

3. A disturbed trial: an obstacle appears

The obstacle came within 1.2 cm of the robot's 0.25 m circle - the chassis itself is smaller so there was no contact. It appears while the robot is driving at up to 0.15 m/s, and the new path has to start from where the robot already is. No spline between the new obstacle and the red box kept the full margin, so the planner fell back to straight legs. That is the planner's designed last resort, and here it is visible.

4. From 104/130 to 130/130 - what the trials changed

The first full run of the explore mission scored 104/130. Every scenario that does not move the robot behind its odometry's back passed. These are the tests that failed:

  • odometry_x3 7/10, push 4/10, combined 4/10: all docked off target, with the EKF 63–335 mm off at the pre-dock pose;
  • push_docking 0/10, 61 mm lateral, as in every route-mission version.

The common cause was due to three combined modules. After the disturbance, the only marker in view near the dock is the dock marker. One marker cannot observe the rotation about it (A), so the estimate arrived at the pre-dock pose wrong. The Week 9 law then docks off the normal because it cannot remove a sideways offset (D). No single module is wrong however where they meet is what caused our pipeline's weakness.

With all three on the same seeds: odometry_x3 went from 7 to 10/10, push from 4 to 10/10, combined from 4 to 10/10, and push_docking from 0 to 10/10.

Earlier development failures were fixed the same way. These were all found through testing:

  • PnP yaw on its own gave a 91 mm/6.9° initial error, and once 1.8 m. Fixed by the wall-line yaw (B).
  • Walls mapped from a wrong pose stayed as ghost walls. Fixed by restarting the map on large corrections.
  • A lost robot waited at the "pre-dock pose" with the dock out of view. Fixed by "find" mode.
  • Markers mapped while lost outvoted the dock marker. Fixed by anchoring, and by dropping them on recovery.
  • Replanning every scan near a goal beside an obstacle caused 598 replans - fixed by hysteresis.
  • A first curvature limit of 5 /m silently turned the spline off: 9 of 10 missions fell back to straight legs. Found by comparing the spline and straight rows, then fixed.

The EKF lock-out recovery came from the "route" mission's trials. A 17 N push and a 0.2 m/15° start error both failed with over 900 correct measurements rejected by the gate because after an unseen jump P is still confident. With the recovery, both were able to dock, and it never fires falsely in nominal, noise, calibration, landmark or obstacle trials.

5. Interpretation

  • Not sensitive to random noise: 4× camera noise and 3× LiDAR noise are indistinguishable from nominal. The filter averages random noise away as do RANSAC and DBSCAN.
  • Calibration error is the most damaging sensor fault: A 2% focal-length error gives NEES 100. It is a systematic scale error on every range (range from apparent size, n = fs/Z), and the filter models only random noise so it trusts biased measurements. Every trial still docked, with 3 mm lateral error, because docking steers on the camera directly. What we learned for B - calibration accuracy matters more than image noise.
  • Lost and recovered: In odometry_x3, push and combined the estimate is lost by up to 0.86–1.05 m mid-route, and the recovery fires 1.3–3.3 times per mission. The robot still docks within 4 mm lateral, because the dock marker and its wall fix the full pose at the pre-dock pose, and back off corrects what remains.
  • Nominal EKF max 224 mm. In one trial the estimate drifted before the dock marker was first seen, then was corrected. That trial still docked.

The explore mission knows less but is more robust, because of three things the route mission does not have: the wall-line heading (full pose from one marker), the dock back-off, and replanning on its own LiDAR map.

A measured trade-off in the route mission: The spline controller scored 107/120, against 110/120 for straight segments on the same route and seeds. All three extra failures were docking off target after bad odometry or a push. The spline reaches the pre-dock pose about 10 s sooner and turns less on the spot, so it collects about a third fewer wall-marker updates (210 against 309) and has less time to re-converge. At 10 trials per scenario a 3-trial difference is within trial-to-trial variation but the mechanism is consistent with the logged data.

Beyond the assignment brief
  • A robustness study of 250 full missions (130 explore and 120 route), repeatable by seed, with automatic failure diagnosis. Development of this study was assisted with Generative AI.
  • Every failure traced to a cause across modules and the fixes measured on the same seeds.
  • Two missions and two controllers compared under identical disturbances.
What these results do and do not support
  • 10 trials per scenario. A 10/10 result means the true success rate is likely above about 70% (95% binomial interval) but does not mean that the system never fails. Differences of 1–3 trials are indicative only.
  • Disturbances are simulated: a push is a 0.2 s force and odometry error is a radius scale plus random slip. Real slip, lighting, motion blur and camera latency were not tested, and neither were moving obstacles or a wrong marker map in simulation.
  • All of this is one room and one marker layout. The wall-line yaw needs the reference marker on a straight wall, and an error in the measured dock offset moves the pre-dock pose by the same amount.
